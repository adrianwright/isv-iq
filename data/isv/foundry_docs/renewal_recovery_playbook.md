# At-Risk Renewal Recovery Playbook

Owner: Customer Success Operations. Applies to strategic and enterprise renewals flagged at risk.
This is a synthetic demo document, written for illustration only.

## 1. When to use this playbook

Start the playbook when a renewing account shows two or more of the following:

- an open P1 incident, or a P1 incident that missed an uptime or response commitment;
- a blocked or late adoption milestone on a major subscription;
- seat adoption below the target for the subscription;
- negative customer sentiment in recent support or meeting records;
- a new executive decision-maker who has not met the account team;
- an overdue invoice or a payment dispute;
- an active competitor evaluation or relevant public signal.

## 2. Principles

An at-risk strategic renewal should have one integrated recovery plan covering service, adoption,
commercial, and relationship signals. Separate teams working separate plans produce conflicting
messages to the customer. One person owns the plan and the customer-facing story.

Recovery is earned by completing commitments, not by announcing them. Every claim to the customer
must be backed by a record.

## 3. Recovery plan structure

### 3.1 Service

Confirm the incident owner, the mitigation in place, the committed remediation date, and what remains
under investigation. Deliver the incident recovery summary. If an uptime or response commitment was
missed, prepare a service-credit recommendation for review under the Premium Support policy.

### 3.2 Adoption

Identify the blocked milestone, the blocker, and the owner. Agree a revised date with the customer.
Where the blocker is a support case, the adoption milestone date depends on the case. State that
dependency openly rather than promising a date the team cannot control.

### 3.3 Commercial

Prepare the renewal proposal and its timeline. Use the Strategic Renewal Pricing and Approval
Policy and the Renewal Approval Matrix for approvals. Do not use pricing to offset unresolved
service risk.

### 3.4 Relationship

Map the decision-makers. If a new executive has joined, request an introduction through the
executive sponsor and prepare a short briefing that leads with the recovery plan, not the renewal.

## 4. The first customer package

The first customer package should include:

1. the incident recovery summary;
2. committed support follow-ups, each with an owner and a date;
3. the adoption recovery milestone and its dependency;
4. the renewal proposal timeline.

Send the package together, not in pieces, so the customer sees one coherent plan.

## 5. Commitments

Open commitments require named owners and dates. Track them in the commitments record. A commitment
marked completed must have evidence (a sent email, a delivered document, or a meeting record).
Review open commitments at every weekly account review.

## 6. Expansion during recovery

Expansion discovery may continue in parallel, but a new product proposal should be positioned as a
validated customer outcome rather than a substitute for remediation. Do not table an expansion
proposal in the same conversation as an unresolved service failure unless the customer raises it.
Expansion value is forecast separately from the renewal.

## 7. Human review gate

Executive outreach, pricing, service credits, roadmap statements, and commercial commitments
require human review before release. Automated assistants may draft these items and must label them
as drafts. The account executive owns release.

## 8. Cadence

| Cadence | Activity |
| --- | --- |
| Daily | Incident update while a P1 is open. |
| Weekly | Account review of commitments, risks, and forecast. |
| Biweekly | Executive sponsor check-in. |
| At milestones | Customer checkpoint with the recovery owner. |

## 9. Exit criteria

The account leaves at-risk status only when all of the following are true and recorded:

- no open P1 incident, or the open incident has a committed remediation the customer has accepted;
- the blocked adoption milestone has a revised, agreed date or is complete;
- the customer's executive decision-maker has engaged on the renewal;
- the proposal has been delivered and approved;
- no material commitment is overdue.

Raise the forecast only after the exit criteria are met, and record the evidence.

## 10. Common mistakes

- Treating proposal delivery as proof of renewal confidence.
- Stating that an incident is resolved when it is only mitigated.
- Promising a service credit before approval.
- Letting expansion enthusiasm raise the renewal forecast.
- Running separate service, adoption, and commercial threads with the customer.
