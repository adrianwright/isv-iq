# Strategic Renewal Pricing and Approval Policy

Owner: Deal Desk and Finance. Applies to strategic and enterprise renewals. This is a synthetic
demo document, written for illustration only.

## 1. Purpose

This policy sets the rules for proposing renewal pricing, multi-year terms, and price protection,
and the approvals required before a proposal is released to a customer. It also defines how the
account team should reflect renewal risk in the forecast.

## 2. Account tiers

| Tier | Typical ARR | Typical renewal term |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strategic | USD 2,000,000 and above | 36 months |
| Enterprise | USD 500,000 to USD 1,999,999 | 12 to 36 months |
| Commercial | Below USD 500,000 | 12 months |

Tier is assigned by the account's segment in the CRM. A strategic account receives an executive
sponsor and a named support escalation manager.

## 3. Multi-year proposals and price protection

Strategic customers may receive a multi-year proposal with price protection when the account team
documents:

1. customer value delivered;
2. renewal risk;
3. payment status;
4. support exposure;
5. executive sponsorship.

Price protection caps the annual uplift for the renewal term. The standard cap is three percent per
year. Any cap lower than three percent requires the Finance approval described below.

## 4. Approval requirements

| Proposal | Required approvers |
| --- | --- |
| Twelve-month renewal at current price | Account owner |
| Multi-year (24 or 36 months) renewal, standard price protection | Account owner, Finance |
| Three-year term | Account owner, Finance, designated executive sponsor |
| Discount greater than ten percent | Account owner, Finance, Sales Director |
| Service credit or commercial concession | Account executive, support leader, Finance |

Approvals must be recorded in the deal desk system before a proposal is released. An approval given
by chat or email is not sufficient until it has been recorded.

## 5. Payment status

An overdue invoice does not automatically block a proposal, but payment timing must be confirmed
before final approval. If an invoice is more than sixty days overdue, Finance must confirm a
payment plan before approving any multi-year term.

## 6. Pricing and service risk

Pricing must not be used to conceal unresolved service risk. When recovery work remains open, the
proposal should state the dependency and preserve a human approval gate. A proposal must not offer
a discount in return for the customer waiving a service-credit entitlement.

## 7. Forecast guidance

Account teams forecast the expected renewal, not the contract value that is expiring. Use these
rules:

- **Commit:** all material risks closed or fully mitigated, executive sponsor engaged, proposal
  approved and delivered. Forecast at the proposed renewal value.
- **Best case:** a credible path to commit with one or two open items that have owners and dates.
- **At risk:** one or more material risks open, such as an open P1 incident with a missed
  commitment, a blocked adoption milestone, or a new executive decision-maker who has not engaged.
  Forecast below current ARR, and state which risks drive the reduction.

A forecast should be raised only when the risks that justified lowering it have closed and the
evidence is recorded. Delivering a proposal on time is not evidence that the customer will sign.
Expansion revenue must not be added to a renewal forecast to offset risk.

## 8. Approval workflow

1. The account owner drafts the proposal and records the risks and dependencies.
2. Finance validates the pricing, discount, and payment status.
3. The executive sponsor reviews strategic proposals and any concession.
4. Deal Desk records the approvals and releases the proposal.
5. The account owner delivers the proposal and logs the delivery.

If any approver declines, the account owner may revise and resubmit. Approvals expire thirty days
after they are recorded.

## 9. Rules for automated assistants

An automated assistant may summarize this policy, compare an account's state against the approval
requirements, and draft a proposal for review. It must not release a proposal, state that an
approval has been granted unless it is recorded, or promise a price.

## 10. Exceptions

Exceptions to this policy require written approval from the Chief Revenue Officer and Finance and
are recorded with a reason.
