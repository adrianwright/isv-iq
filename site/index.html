One churn question, answered from sources nobody keeps together.

An account team has to decide whether Contoso is at risk of churn. The evidence sits in enterprise systems, policy and contracts, communications and public signals. This demo brings those sources together, shows its evidence, and stops at a draft for a person to review.

Two ways to use it

Across a portfolio or in a conversation.

The same evidence-led assessment can support a scheduled batch that processes account records into reports for review, or an interactive question from a person working with an agent. Either way, the result is grounded in sources and stays subject to human review.

Guided reconciliation

The evidence is reconciled by rules, then reviewed by a person.

Evidence from every source lands in one place, and the system settles the assessment. It does not leave that to whoever read the last email. Each source is trusted for what it knows best: enterprise systems for usage, spend and support; policy and contracts for terms and approvals; communications for customer sentiment and promises; public signals for outside changes. Evidence is weighed by category, never averaged.

Records win

An open priority-one case, a falling adoption trend or an overdue invoice caps the verdict at "watch", however upbeat the narrative around it. The conflict is written down, not smoothed over.

The worst signal holds

Evidence can lower a status but never raise it. Confidence falls with each conflict and each source that could not be reached, so a thin answer says so.

The model explains, it does not decide

A model writes the briefing from facts that are already settled. If it changes the verdict or cites evidence that does not exist, its text is discarded and the plain draft is used.

Method

Parallel retrieval, stated uncertainty, a person at the end.

Whether accounts are assessed in a batch or someone asks a question interactively, each assessment follows the same steps.

  1. Interpret the questionResolve the account, the renewal, the churn risk and what is missing.
  2. Retrieve in parallelAsk every connected source at the same time, and time each one.
  3. Guided reconciliationSeparate what is met from blockers, contradictions and gaps.
  4. Draft the next actionReturn citations, an owner and a task that stays unsubmitted.
